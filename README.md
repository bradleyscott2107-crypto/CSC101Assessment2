# CSC101Assessment2
This is the repository for the course CSC101 Assessment 2 
## Reflection

### How I used AI to assist my coding
I used AI as a study partner and a second pair of eyes rather than something to hand work off to. In Week 8 it helped me set up my "My Data Story" notebook in VS Code, which reads and analyses a text file of my favourite movies, and get it into my GitHub `week8` folder. In Week 10 it helped me work through the AI Collaboration notebook, where I analysed `sales_data.csv` and `transactions.csv` and critiqued AI-generated code instead of trusting it. For the "Show Me Your Process" worksheet I used it to plan the tip calculator and late fee problems by hand (understanding the problem, inputs and outputs, working an example) before any code. It also helped me complete the testing and specification worksheet, including the leap year and password validator exercises.

### Challenges I faced
- **Messy data:** the Week 10 CSV files stored dollar amounts with `$` signs, so they loaded as text and calculations failed until they were cleaned.
- **Planning before coding:** it was tempting to jump straight to code, but the Week 10 worksheet required me to understand and solve the problem by hand first.
- **Testing edge cases:** writing specifications and tests for the leap year and password validator exercises meant thinking about unusual inputs, not just the obvious ones.
- **Trusting AI output:** I had to check AI suggestions rather than accept them, which was the point of the critique exercises.

### How I solved them
I stripped the `$` symbols and converted the columns to numbers before analysing them. I worked through examples by hand so I could check the code's output against them. I wrote test cases for boundary inputs, and I reviewed and critiqued AI responses instead of copying them. I documented my process through markdown cells, the AI conversation log and clear GitHub commit messages.
